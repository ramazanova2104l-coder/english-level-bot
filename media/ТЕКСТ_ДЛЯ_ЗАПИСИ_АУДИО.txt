===== general_listen_hard.mp3 (Разговорный английский / Просто интересно) =====
A lot of people assume that the key to learning a language is talent. You've either got it or you haven't. But the research really doesn't back that up. What seems to matter far more is how consistently you're exposed to the language, and, crucially, whether you're actually made to produce it. You can listen to podcasts for years and still freeze the moment someone asks you a question. That's not because you don't know the words. It's because retrieving them under pressure is a separate skill, and it only develops through practice. So if I had to give one piece of advice, it wouldn't be 'study more grammar'. It would be: find situations where you have no choice but to speak, even if you feel a bit uncomfortable at first.

Interviewer: So, Anna, you got one of the highest scores in the country in English. What was your secret?
Anna: Honestly, there wasn't really a secret. I'd say the most important thing was that I started preparing early, in tenth grade, rather than leaving it until the last few months. And I didn't just do practice tests. I actually think a lot of students overdo those. I read books and watched series in English because I enjoyed it, and that helped my vocabulary far more than memorising word lists.
Interviewer: Was there any part of the exam you found particularly difficult?
Anna: The essay, definitely. At first my essays were full of ideas but badly organised. My teacher kept telling me that examiners care about structure just as much as content, and once I started planning each paragraph before writing, my marks went up quite quickly.

===== travel_listen_hard.mp3 (Для путешествий) =====
People often ask me how I manage to travel so much on such a small budget. The honest answer is that I've stopped trying to see everything. When I first started travelling, I'd rush from one famous sight to the next, ticking things off a list, and I'd come home exhausted and, frankly, with very few real memories. These days I'll pick one neighbourhood and stay there for a week. I rent a small flat instead of a hotel, I shop at the local market, and I eat wherever the locals eat. Not only does it work out much cheaper, but I actually get to talk to people. Some of my best travel moments have come from a conversation with a shop owner, not from a museum.

===== work_listen_hard.mp3 (Для работы) =====
One of the biggest mistakes I see new managers make is assuming that their job is to have all the answers. It isn't. In fact, the moment you start solving every problem yourself, your team stops thinking. What I've found works much better is asking questions. What do you think we should do? What options have you considered? At first it feels slower, and frankly, it can be frustrating when you can see the solution and they can't. But within a few months, people start coming to you with solutions rather than problems, and that frees you up to focus on the bigger picture.

For our science project this year, my friend Lily and I decided to find out whether plants grow better with music. We took six identical plants and put three of them in a room where classical music was playing for two hours a day, and the other three in a quiet room. We gave them exactly the same amount of water and light. To be honest, we expected the music plants to grow much faster, but after a month the difference was tiny, so small that our teacher said it could just be a coincidence. We were a bit disappointed at first, but our teacher explained that an unexpected result is still a real result, and that's what science is all about.
